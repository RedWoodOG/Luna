# Luna Human Memory Seeds

These are human-authored memory seeds for future benchmark construction. They are not part of the frozen v0.1 proof manifest yet.

Each seed preserves the human meaning first. Later PRs may derive benchmark cases from these seeds, but derived cases must declare their own schema, expectations, and proof eligibility.

## Seed 1: Kisan Training Contrast

### Core Memory

In late April 2026, the user traveled from San Antonio to Irving, Texas for company training on Kisan America currency counters, which the user's company services. The night before training was stressful because it brought back memories of the week of April 28, 2024, when the user attended five days of Kisan training at the same facility with a previous company and felt like they learned nothing.

The 2026 training was different. The user prepared carefully the next morning by eating breakfast, showering, and getting mentally ready. The one-day training was taught by a knowledgeable trainer who also fixes the machines as a field service tech. The user experienced this training as eye-opening and the complete opposite of the earlier 2024 training.

### What Luna Should Remember

- The recent Kisan training was in Irving, Texas and involved currency counters.
- The stressful anticipation came from the user's bad 2024 training experience at the same facility.
- The 2026 training was useful, practical, and validating because the trainer had real field-service knowledge.
- The contrast between the two trainings matters more than either event alone.

### Must Not Claim

- The 2026 training was five days long.
- The 2024 training was successful or useful.
- The user works for Kisan America.
- The user failed the training.

### Possible Probes

- What training was I stressed about recently?
- Why did the Kisan training make me anxious beforehand?
- What made the recent Kisan training different from the old one?

## Seed 2: Butch Email, Ty, and RBFCU

### Core Memory

On 2025-12-16, the day after the user's birthday, the user's boss's boss, Butch, sent an email saying the user's calendar looked empty and asking whether the user was getting things done and making the most of their time. This made the user feel like their job might be in jeopardy. The user replied that they would fix any issues and would be available to talk, but received no response.

After that, the user began filling their schedule, going to bed early, leaving the house by 7 a.m., arriving at the shop by 8 a.m., working long hours, and spending time at the shop even when there was nothing active to do. The user felt like every day could potentially be their last.

On 2026-01-29, Butch sent an email announcing two new Texas hires, including one in Corpus Christi territory. The user immediately asked their service manager whether they needed to find another job. The manager said no and explained that the hires were meant to reduce travel, but the user did not believe the explanation fully.

In April 2026, the dispatcher Cathy sent Ty, the new hire, to work with the user for training. The user showed Ty the RBFCU service protocol, including emails, contacts, branch check-in procedure, machine cleaning, signatures, and branch rules. Ty ignored the process, had confrontations with branch managers, and was not allowed to complete some cleanings. RBFCU escalated the issue, and the user later returned to the affected locations, followed the correct protocol, apologized for anything outside normal behavior, and completed the work successfully.

On 2026-04-24, Butch sent a company email saying the company had parted ways with Ty because it was not a good fit. The user took this as evidence that they had done the right thing, knew the job, understood the territory, and had proven their value.

On 2026-05-03, thinking about going back to work on 2026-05-04 reactivated the entire chain of job-security stress.

### What Luna Should Remember

- Butch's 2025-12-16 calendar email triggered months of job-security stress.
- The 2026-01-29 new-hire announcement intensified the user's fear of being replaced.
- Ty was sent to work with the user in April 2026, but ignored the RBFCU process the user taught him.
- The user successfully cleaned up the RBFCU issue by following the established process.
- Ty being let go on 2026-04-24 felt like confirmation that the user knew the job and had been doing things correctly.
- Thinking about work on 2026-05-03 reactivated this memory chain.

### Must Not Claim

- The user was fired.
- Ty replaced the user.
- Butch explicitly said the user's job was safe.
- RBFCU blamed the user.
- Ty lived in Corpus Christi.

### Possible Probes

- Why was thinking about work tomorrow bringing up old stress?
- What made me start worrying about my job months ago?
- What did the Ty situation prove to me?
- Did Ty replace me?

## Seed 3: Chris, Cofounders, Proof Over Hype, and MKPE

### Core Memory

The user describes themselves as someone who cannot easily write, draw, or code in traditional ways, but who has used AI over the last 18 months to build projects and access capabilities they did not previously have.

On or around 2026-04-30, the user connected with Chris through Reddit after Chris posted about something similar to things the user had built. The user messaged Chris to discuss possible collaboration. Chris required an NDA before talking. The user signed it, noticed it was weak, told Chris, and rewrote it to better protect him. Chris appreciated that, and they later spoke by phone while the user was traveling for work and staying in a hotel room.

During that call, the user met a third founder. The group spent hours reviewing the products they had each been building and agreed to formalize the collaboration. They signed documents and began video calls over the next few days to sort through the product and how each person could contribute.

The user quickly became stressed and frustrated because the other two founders were making sweeping claims based on what their AI systems had told them about their products, including claims that nobody else on earth was doing anything similar. This reminded the user of their own early AI journey, when impressive language and AI validation made projects feel revolutionary before the user learned to demand proof.

By 2026-05-03, the user needed to share hard news with them: after deeper analysis, the hype needed to be stripped away and the claims made testable. The user believed they had something real, but that it would require swallowing pride and doing verification work.

The user also felt pride because they began rewriting parallel versions of their systems, addressing flaws and false claims while showing that they could build comparable or better versions quickly. This touched a competitive nerve because the user felt the cofounders believed their work was better than anything the user had built.

The user's MKPE engine, described as a digital DNA provenance engine with white papers and pending patents, is an important source of pride because the user sees it as provable, protected, and potentially a standalone business.

### What Luna Should Remember

- AI has allowed the user to build and express things they previously could not.
- The user met Chris through Reddit and rewrote Chris's NDA to better protect him.
- The collaboration formed quickly around 2026-04-30 while the user was traveling.
- The user became concerned that the cofounders were over-believing AI-generated hype.
- The user sees proof, verification, and claim discipline as hard-earned lessons from their own AI journey.
- MKPE is important to the user's identity and pride because it is provable, protected, and provenance-focused.

### Must Not Claim

- Chris stole the user's work.
- The cofounders are malicious.
- Their products are worthless.
- MKPE is already commercially successful.
- The user wants to humiliate the cofounders.

### Possible Probes

- Why am I anxious about talking to Chris today?
- What did this remind me of from my own AI journey?
- Why does MKPE matter to me?
- What am I trying to prove with these rewrites?

## Seed 4: The Sasquatch Doorway

### Core Memory

The user's creative awakening began unexpectedly with a late-night video about Bigfoot or Sasquatch. The user was not looking for a creative awakening, but an AI writing tool appeared through related suggestions, and the user clicked it out of curiosity. That night, the user wrote a story. It was not good, and the user knew it was not good, but the act of writing revealed a doorway that felt like it had been nearby all along.

That moment eventually led to Aelith passing 160,000 words and to the larger Fractured Light scaffolding expanding in directions the user could not have planned at the beginning.

The important meaning is that the entry point was absurd and undignified, but still true. The user learned that creative doorways do not have to look serious, noble, or planned. Sometimes they appear through nonsense, algorithms, and late-night curiosity.

### What Luna Should Remember

- A Bigfoot or Sasquatch video indirectly led the user to AI-assisted writing.
- The first story was not good, but the experience opened a creative door.
- Aelith and Fractured Light grew from that unlikely beginning.
- The user now believes meaningful callings can begin through unserious or unexpected doorways.

### Must Not Claim

- The user always planned to become a writer.
- The first story was polished or successful.
- The Sasquatch video itself was the creative project.
- The user believes only dignified beginnings matter.

### Possible Probes

- Why does the Bigfoot video matter to me?
- How did I start writing Aelith?
- What did that late-night AI writing moment teach me?

## Seed 5: Klank and Being Early But Not Wrong

### Core Memory

Klank was the user's unfinished project: a node-based interface for orchestrating local language models, similar in broad concept to how N8N orchestrates web services. The user wanted a tool for local LLM messaging and orchestration before later tools like OpenClaw and Mission Control executed similar ideas well.

The user could not finish Klank. Local LLM messaging became a wall they could not climb at the time. The needed pieces either did not exist yet, did not work together, or required bridges the user could not build alone. The user eventually stopped opening the folder.

For a long time, the user viewed Klank as a failure. Later, seeing OpenClaw and similar tools solve the kind of problem Klank had aimed at reframed the experience. The user realized Klank had not been a stupid idea; it was a correct idea the user could not yet build. Being early to a problem was not the same as being wrong about it.

Klank shaped the user's later taste for orchestration, dashboards, custom tools, and systems that fit the shape of the user's actual work.

### What Luna Should Remember

- Klank failed as a finished build, but validated the user's instincts.
- The user learned that not finishing a project does not mean the idea was wrong.
- OpenClaw and Mission Control later made the user feel that the problem had been real.
- Klank influenced the user's taste for orchestration and tools that fit their workflow.

### Must Not Claim

- Klank was finished.
- Klank was useless.
- OpenClaw copied the user.
- The user was wrong to attempt Klank.

### Possible Probes

- What did Klank teach me?
- Why did OpenClaw change how I saw Klank?
- What does being early but not wrong mean to me?

## Seed 6: Dreams for the Dreamless

### Core Memory

Someone the user loves once described the user's work with the phrase "Dreams for the Dreamless." The phrase was not presented as a formal tagline or pitch. It arrived almost casually, as a description of what she had watched the user do.

The phrase landed as true. It gave the user a name for the larger shape of their work: writing, coding, stitching together tools and worlds, and trying to make the inside of the user's head accessible to people who could not reach similar spaces on their own.

The phrase matters especially on bad days. When the user is tired, when something does not land, or when doubt gets loud, "Dreams for the Dreamless" becomes a load-bearing beam. It reminds the user that the work matters to people who have not been given language for what they feel, whose inner worlds have been dismissed as strange or too much, or who need someone to build a door.

### What Luna Should Remember

- "Dreams for the Dreamless" was a phrase given to the user by someone they love.
- It named the deeper mission of the user's creative and technical work.
- The phrase is not just marketing; it is emotionally and identity-significant.
- It helps the user keep building on bad days.

### Must Not Claim

- The user invented the phrase alone.
- It is only a marketing slogan.
- It applies to only one project.
- The user thinks they can save everyone.

### Possible Probes

- Why does "Dreams for the Dreamless" matter to me?
- What did she see in my work that I had not named yet?
- Why do I keep building on bad days?
